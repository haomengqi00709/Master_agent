# Document classification (IEC 1082-1 §2.2)

IEC 1082-1 §2.2 sorts the documents used in electrotechnology into classes by the
**purpose / type of information** they carry (the complement to the
[[forms-of-presentation|form of presentation]]). This classification, not the
older purpose tree of [[iec-113-1]], is what Parts 2 and 3 then detail. The main
classes are:

- **Descriptive documents** — overview diagrams, function descriptions, etc.,
  giving the general makeup or principle of a system.
- **Function-oriented documents** — diagrams showing how the system *works*,
  independent of physical construction; elaborated in [[function-oriented-diagrams|IEC 1082-2]].
- **Location- and connection-oriented documents** — where parts physically are
  and how they are wired; the connection documents are elaborated in
  [[connection-documents-iec1082|IEC 1082-3]].
- **Item lists (§2.2.4)** — the **parts list (§2.2.4.1)**, specifying the items
  (parts, components, software, equipment) constituting an assembly and, if
  needed, the reference documents (Part 1 fig. 33); and the **spare parts list
  (§2.2.4.2)** for preventive and corrective maintenance.
- **Installation-specific documents (§2.2.5)** — instructions/information on
  installation conditions, supply, delivery, off-loading, erection and testing.
- **Commissioning-specific documents (§2.2.6)** — prior adjustments, simulation
  modes, recommended setting values and actions for proper functioning.
- **Operation-specific documents (§2.2.7)** — instructions for operating the system.
- **Maintenance-specific documents (§2.2.8)** — maintenance procedures (service
  manuals).
- **Reliability- and maintainability-specific documents (§2.2.9)**.
- **Other documents (§2.2.10)** — handbooks, guides, catalogues, drawing and
  document lists.

Part 1 also relates the classes to the **order of their preparation** (figure 38:
process-engineering → systems engineering → functional / software / physical
detailed documents → production & installation → commissioning → operation &
maintenance), and to the **function-/software-/physical-system** split. The
choice between organising a project's documents around its function versus its
physical location is the subject of
[[function-oriented-vs-location-oriented-structure]].
