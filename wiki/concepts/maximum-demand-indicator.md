# Maximum demand indicator (MDI)

The **attachment to a meter that indicates the highest value of the average power
utilized during successive equal intervals of time** (IEC 387 §3.8). A meter
fitted with one becomes a [[maximum-demand-meter]] (§3.9). The MDI integrates
power over a fixed interval (e.g. 15 min) and retains the peak; the underlying
metrology standard is **IEC 211** (Maximum demand indicators, class 1,0),
cited in §2 and §9 c.

Two coefficients characterise an MDI's reading, defined separately on
[[reading-factor-and-constant-mdi]]: the **reading factor C** (§3.20, the factor
by which the reading in units of power must be multiplied to obtain the
corresponding power in the same units) and the **constant K** (§3.21, the
coefficient by which the reading must be multiplied to obtain the value of the
corresponding active or reactive power).

Marking (§9 c, [[tariff-device-symbols]]): a single-pointer indicator needs no
symbol; cumulative and warning-device variants and the drum type each have a
Table 6 example with multiplier, integration period and detent time (e.g.
0,2 kW/div, 15 min, 9 s). The static-display demand symbols 9.6–9.7 (actual and
highest average demand for the billing period) apply to static MDIs.
