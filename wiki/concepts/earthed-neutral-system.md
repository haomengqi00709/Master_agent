# Earthed neutral system

An earthed neutral system is one in which the neutral is connected to earth, either solidly, or through a resistance or reactance of a value low enough to reduce materially any transient oscillations and to improve the conditions for selective earth-fault protection ([[iec-56]] 3.101.7). By providing a low-impedance neutral path, it holds the sound phases close to their normal phase-to-earth voltage during an earth fault, yielding a comparatively low [[earth-fault-factor]] — not exceeding 1.4 when the zero-sequence reactance is below three times the positive-sequence reactance and zero-sequence resistance does not exceed positive-sequence reactance ([[iec-56]] 3.101.8). This contrasts with the [[isolated-neutral-system]] and the [[resonant-earthed-system]], both of which limit earth-fault current but allow sound-phase voltages to rise toward the square root of 3. The lower earth-fault factor permits reduced [[external-insulation]] and [[rated-insulation-level]] for the same nominal system voltage.
