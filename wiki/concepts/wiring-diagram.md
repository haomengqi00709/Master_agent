# Wiring diagram

The third purpose-class of [[diagram]] in [[iec-113-1]] (§5); the U.S.A. term is
**connection diagram**. Wiring diagrams are **intended to guide the making and
checking of the connections of an installation or equipment**. For an equipment
they show the **internal or external connections, or both**. They may sometimes
also show the layout of the different parts and accessories — such as terminal
blocks — and the wiring between them (§5).

Unlike an [[explanatory-diagram]] (which explains *function*), a wiring diagram is
practical: it supports the physical task of wiring and verifying connections. IEC
113-1 defines **three subtypes** by scope (§5):

- [[unit-wiring-diagram]] (§5.1) — all connections **within** a unit.
- [[interconnection-diagram]] (§5.2) — the connections **between** the different
  units of an installation.
- [[terminal-diagram]] (§5.3) — the terminals and the internal/external conductors
  connected to them.

A note to §5 states that **any of the wiring diagrams can be replaced or
supplemented by a [[table-document]]** (a wiring table). A wiring diagram is a
common candidate for [[topographical-representation]] (§9), since the layout of
parts is often relevant, and the same drawing may serve as both an explanatory and
a wiring diagram, forming a [[mixed-diagram]] (Note to §3–6).
