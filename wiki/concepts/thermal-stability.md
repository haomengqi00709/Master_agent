# Thermal stability of an arrester (§2.41)

An arrester is **thermally stable if, after an operating duty causing temperature
rise, the temperature of the resistor elements decreases with time when the
arrester is energized at specified [[continuous-operating-voltage]] and at
specified ambient conditions** ([[iec-99-4]] §2.41). It is the desired condition —
the opposite of [[thermal-runaway]] — and the principal pass criterion of the
[[operating-duty-test]].

Thermal stability is verified per the [[thermal-stability-evaluation]] (§7.5.6):
the arrester section passes if the peak of the resistive component of leakage
[[continuous-current|current]], or the power dissipation, or the resistor
temperature **steadily decreases** during at least the last 15 min of the elevated
continuous-operating-voltage (U_c\*) application in the duty-test sequence (figures
1, 2, C.1). If the trend is unclear, the U_c\* application is extended until a
steady decrease is confirmed; if no increasing trend appears within 3 h, the
section is considered stable. The arrester also passes only if the
[[residual-voltage]] before/after has not changed by more than 5 % and examination
reveals no puncture, [[impulse-waveshape-terminology|flashover]] or cracking of the
[[metal-oxide-resistor|metal-oxide resistors]]. A separate optional **special
thermal stability test** (§5.7, §8.2.2) may be agreed, run on three different
sections from routine production.
