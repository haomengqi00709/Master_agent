# Surge arrester (lightning arrester / surge diverter)

A **lightning arrester (surge diverter)** is a device designed to protect electrical apparatus from
high transient voltage and to limit the duration, and frequently the amplitude, of follow current
([[iec-99-1]] §3). The term includes any external series gap essential to the proper functioning of
the device as installed, whether or not supplied as an integral part. Arresters are usually connected
between the electrical conductors of a network and earth, though they may be connected across the
windings of apparatus or between conductors. Within IEC 99-1 the dominant construction is the
[[non-linear-resistor-type-arrester|non-linear resistor (valve) type]] (§4): one or more
[[series-gap|series gaps]] in series with one or more [[non-linear-series-resistor|non-linear
resistors]]. Under a surge the gaps spark over and the resistor passes the [[discharge-current]] at a
limited [[residual-voltage]]; the resistor's high resistance at power frequency then limits the
[[follow-current]] so the gaps can reseal.

Two structural sub-divisions are defined. A **section** (§7) is a complete, suitably housed part
including series gaps and non-linear resistors in such proportion as is necessary to represent the
behaviour of a complete arrester for a particular test — many type tests are made on a section
because complete high-voltage arresters exceed test-facility capacity. A **unit** (§8) is a completely
housed part that may be connected in series with other units to construct an arrester of higher
voltage rating; a unit is not necessarily a section. The arrester is rated by its
[[rated-voltage-arrester|rated voltage]] and classified by its [[nominal-discharge-current]]. The
graphical symbol is [[07-22-03]] in [[60617-7]] §22.
