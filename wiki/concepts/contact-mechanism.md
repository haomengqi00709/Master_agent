# Contact mechanism — geometry, drive and revolutions

How the two electrodes are arranged and driven inside the
[[spark-test-apparatus]] ([[iec-79-3]] §4.2, Figure 4). The
[[tungsten-wire-electrode|wire holder]] rotates so that the four tungsten wires
**slide over the slotted [[cadmium-disc-electrode|cadmium disc]]**. The **distance
between the wire holder and the cadmium disc is 10 mm**; the **free length of the
tungsten wires is 117 mm**; the wires are straight and normal to the disc surface
when not in contact.

**Drive and gearing.** The **axes of the two driving shafts are 31 mm apart**, are
**electrically insulated from each other and from the base-plate**, and the test
current is led in and out through **sliding contacts** on the shafts. The shafts
are **geared together by non-conductive gears with a ratio of 50:12**. The wire
holder is driven by an electric motor (with reduction gearing if needed) at
**80 rev/min**; the cadmium disc therefore turns in the **opposite direction at
19,2 rev/min** (80 × 12/50 = 19,2). **Gas-tight bearings** in the base-plate are
required unless a gas-flow system is used.

**Counting the operations.** A **counting device records the number of
revolutions of the motor-driven wire-holder shaft** (the basis for the "defined
number of operations" in [[spark-ignition-test-principle]]); alternatively a
timing device sets the test duration, from which the revolution count is
calculated. The drive (or at least the counter) is best stopped automatically on
ignition — e.g. by a photocell or an explosion-pressure switch (Figs 5–6).
Reducing the drive speed is one way to test circuits with large time constants,
but it may change the apparatus's sensitivity (see
[[spark-test-apparatus-limitations]]).
