# Maximum demand meter & indicator

IEC 387 defines two linked terms (§3.8–3.9). A **maximum demand indicator (for a
meter)** is an *attachment to a meter to indicate the highest value of the
average power utilized during successive equal intervals of time* (§3.8); a
**maximum demand meter** is simply *a meter fitted with a maximum demand
indicator* (§3.9). The averaging interval makes this a demand- rather than
energy-quantity device.

Marking is governed by §9 c (tariff devices): a meter with a maximum demand
indicator fitted with **one pointer requires no symbol** — the markings are those
recommended in **IEC 211**. An indicator of the cumulative type has its
cumulative register marked with the appropriate unit of power. Maximum demand
indicators are marked beside the register with the maximum value of the average
power measured and the appropriate symbol. Table 6 gives examples: a **drum-type
maximum demand indicator** (multiplier e.g. 0,2 kW/div, integration period
15 min, detent time 9 s) and a **pointer- or drum-type indicator fitted with a
warning device**. Symbols 9.6 and 9.7 — the instantaneous (actual) average-demand
value and the highest average demand value for the present cumulation (billing)
period — are assigned for static maximum-demand devices with a display.

The associated reading factor C and constant K are defined in
[[reading-factor-and-constant-mdi]] (§3.20–3.21). See [[tariff-device-symbols]]
and the related [[excess-energy-meter]]. General graphical equivalent:
[[08-04-14]] (watt-hour meter with maximum demand recorder).
