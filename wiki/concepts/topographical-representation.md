# Topographical representation

The third axis of the *classification according to method of representation*
(Section Three) in [[iec-113-1]] §9. In topographical representation, **the
positions of the symbols on the diagram correspond wholly or partly to the
topographical (physical) location of the items represented** (§9). The drawing's
geometry thus carries spatial information: where a symbol sits on the sheet
reflects where the real device sits in the installation.

IEC 113-1 lists examples where it can be used (§9): **wiring diagrams**,
**architectural diagrams**, and **network diagrams**. It is therefore strongly
associated with the [[wiring-diagram]] family (§5) and the
[[location-diagram-or-table]] (§6), whose concern is precisely physical layout —
though, per §6, a location diagram need not be to scale, and §9 likewise speaks of
correspondence "wholly or partly." Topographical representation is one of the three
representation methods (§7–9) alongside the conductor-count methods (§7:
[[single-line-representation]], [[multi-line-representation]]) and the arrangement
methods (§8: [[assembled-representation]], [[semi-assembled-representation]],
[[detached-representation]]); per the Note to §7–9, several of these methods may be
used on the same diagram (see [[mixed-diagram]]).
