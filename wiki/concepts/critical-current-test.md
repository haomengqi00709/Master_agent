# Critical-current test

Critical current tests are short-circuit tests additional to the basic test-duties of [[iec-56]] 6.106, applicable only to circuit-breakers whose critical current is below 10% of the rated short-circuit breaking current — the case if average arcing time in Test-duty No. 1 is significantly greater than in Test-duty No. 2 ([[iec-56]] 6.107.1). The critical current is the current, well below rated, that yields the longest arcing time and thus the most onerous thermal duty on the interrupter. Tests are made at currents of 4%-6% and 2%-3% of rated short-circuit breaking current ([[iec-56]] 6.107.2). The duties follow Test-duty No. 1 ([[test-duty]]), but the [[transient-recovery-voltage]] time t3 is modified by the factor sqrt(10/X), X being the test current as a percentage of rated. Small inductive currents are covered separately by [[magnetizing-and-small-inductive-current-switching-test]].
