# Oil circuit-breaker

An oil circuit-breaker is a [[circuit-breaker]] in which the contacts open and close in oil ([[iec-56]] 3.102.7, IEV 441-14-28). The arc drawn between the separating contacts decomposes the surrounding oil, generating hydrogen-rich gas and pressure that cool and deionise the arc, so the oil is both the arc-extinguishing and the insulating medium. Designs range from bulk-oil types, frequently built as a [[dead-tank-circuit-breaker]], to minimum-oil types. It contrasts with the gas-interrupting [[air-blast-circuit-breaker]], [[gas-blast-circuit-breaker]] and [[sf6-circuit-breaker]], and with the [[vacuum-circuit-breaker]] and [[air-circuit-breaker]]. Each [[pole]] carries rated current continuously through its [[main-contact]] and commutates the arc to the [[arcing-contact]] on opening, the arcing contact being sacrificial to protect the main contact from arc erosion.
