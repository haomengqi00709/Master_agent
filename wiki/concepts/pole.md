# Pole (of a circuit-breaker)

A pole of a circuit-breaker is the portion associated exclusively with one electrically separated conducting path of the main circuit and excluding those portions which provide a means for mounting and operating all poles together ([[iec-56]] 3.103.1, IEV 441-15-01). In a three-phase [[circuit-breaker]] there is one pole per phase, each being an electrically independent unit through which one line conductor passes. A pole contains the relevant [[main-circuit]] conducting path, including its [[main-contact]] and [[arcing-contact]], and, in higher-voltage designs, may be built up from one or more [[module]] assemblies stacked in series. The number of poles is a basic rating of the breaker. Poles are commonly operated together by a common mechanism, the shared mounting and operating means being explicitly excluded from the pole itself.
