# Isolated neutral system

An isolated neutral system is a power system that has no intentional connection to earth, except through indicating, measuring or protective devices of very high impedance ([[iec-56]] 3.101.5). Because there is no deliberate low-impedance neutral-to-earth path, single phase-to-earth faults drive only small capacitive currents through the line-to-earth capacitances rather than large fault currents. The trade-off is that the sound (healthy) phases rise close to full line-to-line voltage relative to earth during an earth fault, giving a high [[earth-fault-factor]] approaching the square root of 3. This contrasts with the [[earthed-neutral-system]], where the neutral is deliberately bonded to earth, and with the [[resonant-earthed-system]], which uses an arc-suppression coil. The earthing arrangement directly governs the [[earth-fault-factor]] and hence the [[external-insulation]] and [[internal-insulation]] stresses the circuit-breaker must withstand.
