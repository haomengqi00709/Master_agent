# Tungsten wire electrode

The other electrode of the [[spark-test-apparatus]] consists of **four tungsten
wires of 0,2 mm diameter**, clamped on a **circle of 50 mm diameter** to a wire
holder, as illustrated in the standard's Figure 3 ([[iec-79-3]] §4.2). A note
recommends **rounding off the corners of the holder slightly** at the clamping
points to avoid premature breakage of the wires at the sharp edge.

In the assembled [[contact-mechanism]] (Fig. 4) the wire holder rotates so the
tungsten wires **slide over the slotted [[cadmium-disc-electrode|cadmium disc]]**,
generating the make- and break-sparks. The wires are **straight and fitted normal
to the disc surface** (when not in contact), with a **free length of 117 mm**, set
**10 mm** from the disc. Tungsten is very brittle and tends to split at the ends
after short operation, so the wires are specially prepared — by **fusion** (which
forms a small removable sphere on each end and extends wire life beyond ~25 000
revolutions of the holder) or by a **shearing cut** — per Annex A.1 (see
[[tungsten-wire-preparation]]). For **capacitive circuits only**, two or three of
the wires may be removed to handle large time constants (see
[[spark-test-apparatus-limitations]]). Note also that excessive test current
(beyond ~2,5–3 A) can heat the wires enough to cause additional ignition effects
that invalidate the test.
