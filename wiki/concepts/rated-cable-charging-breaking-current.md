# Rated cable-charging breaking current

Clause 4.108 of [[iec-56]] defines the rated cable-charging breaking current as the maximum cable-charging (capacitive) current the circuit-breaker shall be capable of breaking at its rated voltage under the prescribed conditions of use and behaviour, without exceeding the appropriate maximum permissible switching overvoltages specified by the manufacturer (suggested values in Table IX). It represents switching off an unloaded insulated cable operating at no load, the analogue for cables of the [[rated-line-charging-breaking-current]]. Specifying it is not mandatory but is made on request, and is considered unnecessary for breakers of rated voltage at or below 24 kV; if assigned, values should follow Table VIII (e.g. 10 A at 3.6 kV rising to 500 A at 525 kV). A Table VIII note states these cover the normal maximum needs of most power systems, with higher currents subject to special agreement. Because cable capacitance is large, [[restrike]] and the resulting [[recovery-voltage]] escalation dominate; verified by the [[capacitive-current-switching-test]] ([[iec-56]] 4.108).
