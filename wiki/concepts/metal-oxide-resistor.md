# Non-linear metal-oxide resistor

The non-linear metal-oxide resistor is **the part of the surge arrester which, by
its non-linear voltage-versus-current characteristic, acts as a low resistance to
overvoltages — thus limiting the voltage across the arrester terminals — and as a
high resistance at normal power-frequency voltage** ([[iec-99-4]] §2.2). It is the
active element of every [[metal-oxide-surge-arrester]]: the resistor "blocks" at
[[continuous-operating-voltage|continuous operating voltage]] (drawing only a
small [[continuous-current|continuous current]]) yet conducts heavily during a
surge, clamping the [[residual-voltage|residual voltage]].

Because there is no series spark gap, these resistors are energized continuously
and must withstand long-term ageing — verified by the [[accelerated-ageing-procedure]]
(§7.5.2). The resistors are commonly built into discs (the [[reference-current]]
note cites 0,05–1,0 mA/cm² of disc area), assembled into columns that may be
connected in parallel ([[current-distribution-multi-column]]) and stacked into
[[arrester-unit|units]]. Their energy-handling capability is graded by
[[line-discharge-class]] and verified by the
[[long-duration-current-impulse-withstand-test]] and [[operating-duty-test]];
after those tests the measured residual voltage must not have changed by more than
5 %, and visual examination must reveal no puncture, [[impulse-waveshape-terminology|flashover]],
cracking or other significant damage of the metal-oxide resistors.
