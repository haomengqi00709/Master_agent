# Dead-tank circuit-breaker

A dead tank circuit-breaker is a [[circuit-breaker]] in which a vessel(s) at earth potential surrounds and contains the interrupter(s) and the insulating medium ([[iec-56]] 3.102.4, IEV 441-14-25). The defining characteristic is that the enclosing tank is held at earth (ground) potential, so the interrupting unit sits inside an earthed housing rather than being elevated on insulators. Bushings carry the conductors through the earthed tank to the line terminals. This contrasts directly with the [[live-tank-circuit-breaker]], where the interrupter enclosure is at line potential. The dead-tank construction is common where current transformers are mounted on the bushings and is typical of many oil and [[sf6-circuit-breaker]] designs. Like all [[circuit-breaker]] types it carries continuous current through the [[main-contact]] and commutates the arc to the [[arcing-contact]] of each [[pole]].
