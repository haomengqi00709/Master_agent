# Push-button colour table (Table II)

**IEC 73 §5.5, Table II** — the recommended/compulsory applications of colours for
[[push-button]]s, consolidating the requirements of §5.1–5.4. Surface colours seen
in incident white light; the seven-colour palette of §3.1.2 applies.

| Colour | Meaning | Typical applications |
|--------|---------|----------------------|
| **RED** | Action in case of emergency | Emergency stop; fire-fighting |
| **RED** | STOP or OFF | General stop; stop one/more motors; stop a part of a machine; open a switching device; reset combined with STOP |
| **YELLOW** | Intervention | Intervention to suppress abnormal conditions or to avoid unwanted changes |
| **GREEN** | START or ON | General start; start one/more motors; start a part of a machine; close a switching device |
| **BLUE** | Any specific meaning not covered by the above colours | A meaning not covered by red, yellow and green, allocated in particular cases |
| **BLACK / GREY / WHITE** | No specific meaning assigned | May be used for any function **except** buttons with the sole function of STOP or OFF |

Key consequences: RED is **reserved** for stop/emergency
([[push-button-stop-emergency]], §5.1) and is the only colour with *two* meaning
rows; GREEN is the **preferred** START/ON colour ([[push-button-start-on]], §5.2);
YELLOW = intervention; BLUE = assignable specific meaning; and the neutral
BLACK/GREY/WHITE group is for any function but **not** a sole STOP/OFF — which is
why alternating and inching buttons ([[push-button-start-stop-combined]], §5.3)
and plain RESET buttons ([[push-button-reset]], §5.4) draw from it. For an
[[illuminated-push-button]] this Table governs the **function** of the button
while Table I governs its **lighted colour** (§6.2).
