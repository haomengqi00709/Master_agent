# Rated voltage of an arrester (Uᵣ)

The rated voltage of a metal-oxide arrester is **the maximum permissible r.m.s.
value of power-frequency voltage between its terminals at which it is designed to
operate correctly under temporary overvoltage conditions, as established in the
[[operating-duty-test|operating duty tests]] (§7.5)** ([[iec-99-4]] §2.8). It is
the reference parameter for specifying the arrester's operating characteristics.
A key note (§2.8) clarifies that in 99-4 the rated voltage is specifically the
**10 s power-frequency voltage** applied in the operating-duty test after the high
current or long-duration impulses — and warns that the method used in
[[iec-99-1]] (repetitive impulses at nominal current with power-frequency voltage
applied) does **not necessarily** yield an equivalent value, a discrepancy then
under consideration.

Rated voltage is load-bearing throughout the standard. Standard values follow
[[standard-rated-voltages]] (§4.1, Table 2), in equal steps depending on the
range. It sets the rated-voltage band per [[arrester-classification]] (Table 1).
In testing it is divided by the number of sections n to give the per-section
power-frequency voltage U_r/n (§7.5.1), then raised to the **elevated rated
voltage Uᵣ\*** by the [[accelerated-ageing-procedure]] (§7.5.2.2) and applied for
10 s in the operating-duty test. It also feeds the corrected continuous voltage
U_ct = √2·U_c·(1 + 0,05 L) and the [[line-discharge-class]] charging-voltage and
energy formulas (Annex E). Contrast with [[continuous-operating-voltage]] (U_c),
the *continuous* permissible voltage. (Distinct from the circuit-breaker
[[rated-voltage]] of [[iec-56]].)
