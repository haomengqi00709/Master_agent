# Test flask (200 ml Erlenmeyer)

The reaction vessel of the [[iec-79-4]] method (§4.1). The test flask **shall be
a 200 ml Erlenmeyer flask of borosilicate glass**. A **chemically clean flask
shall be used** for tests on each product and for the final series of tests —
cleanliness matters because surface contamination is one of the
[[factors-affecting-ignition-temperature|factors that can shift the result]].

**Material substitution rule.** Where the [[ignition-temperature]] of the test
sample **exceeds the softening point of a borosilicate glass flask**, or where
the sample would **cause deterioration of such a flask (e.g. by chemical
attack)**, a **quartz or metal flask may be used, provided this is declared in
the test report** (§4.1). This declaration is required because the flask material
and surface are part of the test geometry and can themselves affect ignition.

The flask is **open** and contains ordinary air; the product is injected as
droplets (liquids) or by gas-tight syringe (gases) into its centre via
[[ignition-test-sample-introduction|the sample-introduction apparatus]] (§4.4),
taking care not to wet the walls. It sits in the [[ignition-test-furnace|hot-air
furnace]] (§4.2), which heats it "in an adequately uniform manner"; the
[[ignition-test-thermocouples|thermocouples]] (§4.3) are held against its outer
surface to read the flask temperature, and an observation mirror is recommended
~250 mm above it (§4.6). The Appendix-A furnaces are dimensioned around this flask
(e.g. the flask guide ring and a 300 W neck heater plus 300 W base heater for the
Fig. 1–6 furnace).
