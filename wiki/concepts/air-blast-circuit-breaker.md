# Air-blast circuit-breaker

An air-blast circuit-breaker is a [[circuit-breaker]] in which the arc is extinguished by a blast of compressed air ([[iec-56]] 3.102.11, IEV 441-14-32). The distinguishing feature is a high-pressure air reservoir that releases a powerful blast across the separating contacts at opening, cooling and deionising the arc for interruption at current zero; the same air often provides insulation and operating energy. It is a specific type of [[gas-blast-circuit-breaker]] (3.102.9) using air as the blast gas, the direct counterpart of the [[sf6-circuit-breaker]] which uses sulphur hexafluoride. It must not be confused with the [[air-circuit-breaker]] (3.102.6), where contacts simply open in atmospheric air without a blast. Each [[pole]] carries current through its [[main-contact]] and clears the arc on its [[arcing-contact]].
