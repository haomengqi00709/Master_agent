# Meter constant

The **value expressing the relation between the energy registered by the meter
and the corresponding value of the test output** (IEC 387 §3.19). It is the
scaling figure used to verify a meter against a reference and to relate disc
revolutions or output pulses to energy.

How it is expressed depends on the measurement element (§3.19, §7.2–7.3):

- For an **[[induction-meter]]** it is usually given either in **revolutions of
  the rotor per measuring unit** or in measuring units per revolution — e.g.
  500 rev/kWh, or 2 Wh/rev.
- For a **[[static-energy-meter]]**, where the test output is a pulse train, it
  is given in **pulses per measuring unit** or measuring units per pulse — e.g.
  500 imp/kWh, or 2 Wh/imp.

The meter constant is one of the three items in the Table 4 marking set
([[accuracy-class-and-protective-class]], §7), marked on the name-plate or dial
([[meter-name-plate-and-dial]]). It is distinct from the
[[reading-factor-and-constant-mdi|reading factor C and constant K]] of a maximum
demand indicator (§3.20–3.21), which scale a demand reading rather than a
registered energy. Where a meter is fed via instrument transformers, the
register's transformer factor ([[transformer-operated-meter-marking]], §8) is
applied on top of the meter constant.
