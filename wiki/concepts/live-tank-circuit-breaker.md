# Live-tank circuit-breaker

A live tank circuit-breaker is a [[circuit-breaker]] in which a vessel(s) containing the interrupter(s) is at a potential above earth ([[iec-56]] 3.102.5, IEV 441-14-26). Its distinguishing feature is that the interrupter housing is energised at line potential and is supported on post-insulators that provide insulation to earth, so the tank itself is "live." This is the opposite arrangement to the [[dead-tank-circuit-breaker]], whose enclosure is at earth potential. Live-tank construction is lighter, uses less insulating medium, and is widely used for [[air-blast-circuit-breaker]] and [[sf6-circuit-breaker]] designs at transmission voltages, often built up from a [[module]] stack per [[pole]]. Each pole conducts continuous current through its [[main-contact]] and transfers the arc to the [[arcing-contact]] during interruption.
