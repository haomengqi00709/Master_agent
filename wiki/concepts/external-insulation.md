# External insulation

External insulation comprises the distances in air and the surfaces of solid insulation of the equipment in contact with open air, which are subject to dielectric stresses and to the effects of atmospheric and other external conditions such as pollution, humidity, vermin, etc. ([[iec-56]] 3.101.20). Because it is exposed to the environment, its dielectric strength is influenced by air density, moisture and surface contamination, and discharges across it are typically flashover (over a solid surface) or sparkover (in air). It is therefore generally [[self-restoring-insulation]], recovering full insulating strength after a disruptive discharge, in contrast to the [[internal-insulation]] which is shielded from external conditions and may be [[non-self-restoring-insulation]]. Together external and internal insulation define the dielectric withstand expressed by the [[rated-insulation-level]] of the circuit-breaker.
