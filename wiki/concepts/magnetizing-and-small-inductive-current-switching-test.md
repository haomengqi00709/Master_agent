# Magnetizing and small inductive current switching test

These tests address breaking small inductive currents — transformer magnetizing current, reactor and motor currents — where premature current chopping before natural current zero can excite the load inductance and capacitance into high [[transient-recovery-voltage]] overvoltages. For circuit-breakers rated 100 kV and above, experience shows overvoltages when interrupting unloaded-transformer steady-state magnetizing current at or below rated voltage are small, so no tests are specified; inrush magnetizing current switching is not a normal service condition and is likewise untested ([[iec-56]] 6.112.1). Below 100 kV tests are generally not required, but in cases of doubt should be made on the system under actual service conditions, or three-phase in a laboratory using the actual transformer, the source having as low a capacitance as possible without exceeding the rated TRV ([[iec-56]] 6.112.2). Transformers with reactor-loaded tertiaries, shunt reactors and HV motors are treated as special cases by agreement or are under consideration ([[iec-56]] 6.112.3-6.112.5).
