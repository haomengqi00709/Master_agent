# Access probe and object probe

Two test-probe concepts defined in [[iec-529]] 2nd ed. (clauses 3.8–3.10) that
underpin the [[ip-first-numeral-tests|first-numeral tests]]:

- **Access probe** (§3.8): a test probe simulating, *in a conventional manner*, a
  part of a person (back of hand, finger) or a tool/wire held by a person, used
  to verify **adequate clearance from hazardous parts**. The probe represents the
  body part, not a real one — passing means a person cannot reach a hazardous
  part with that body part/tool.
- **Object probe** (§3.9): a test probe simulating a **solid foreign object**,
  used to verify whether ingress into the enclosure is possible (the object-side
  of the first numeral).
- **Opening** (§3.10): a gap or aperture in an enclosure which exists, or may be
  formed, by applying a test probe at the specified force.

The standardised access/object probes (Table VI, with figures 440/89–443/89), one
per first-numeral level and per [[ip-additional-letter|additional letter]], are:

| Numeral / letter | Probe | Diameter | Test force |
|------------------|-------|----------|-----------|
| 1 / A | rigid **sphere** | 50 mm | 50 N ± 10% |
| 2 / B | jointed **test finger** | 12 mm × 80 mm long | 10 N ± 10% |
| 3 / C | rigid **test rod** | 2,5 mm × 100 mm long | 3 N ± 10% |
| 4 / D | rigid **test wire** | 1,0 mm × 100 mm long | 1 N ± 10% |

Probes are metal where they simulate the conductive reach (with insulating
handle/guard); rod and wire ends are free from burrs and applied at right angles.
The **jointed test finger** (1st-ed. Figure 1) has both joints bendable through
90° in one and the same direction only, with a 50 × 20 mm stop face. "Adequate
clearance" means the probe does not touch live or dangerous moving parts (smooth
rotating shafts are not dangerous); for low-voltage gear a 40 V lamp circuit
between probe and live parts must not light, and for high-voltage gear a
dielectric/clearance check is used (1st ed. §6.1).
