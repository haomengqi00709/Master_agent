# Air circuit-breaker

An air circuit-breaker is a [[circuit-breaker]] in which the contacts open and close in air at atmospheric pressure ([[iec-56]] 3.102.6, IEV 441-14-27). The distinguishing feature is that interruption and the dielectric recovery occur in ordinary atmospheric-pressure air, typically aided by arc-control devices such as arc chutes and magnetic blow-out, rather than by a compressed gas blast or a liquid. It should not be confused with the [[air-blast-circuit-breaker]] (3.102.11), which extinguishes the arc with a blast of compressed air, nor with the [[gas-blast-circuit-breaker]] family. Air circuit-breakers are mainly used at lower and medium voltages. As with every [[circuit-breaker]], continuous current flows through the [[main-contact]] of each [[pole]], while the [[arcing-contact]] takes and clears the arc as the contacts separate.
