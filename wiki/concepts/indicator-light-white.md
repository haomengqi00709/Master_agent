# Indicator light — WHITE (no specific meaning / neutral)

**IEC 73 §4.2, Table I.** A **WHITE** [[indicator-light]] has **no specific meaning
assigned** — it is the **neutral** colour. White may be used for **any meaning**,
and in particular *whenever doubt exists about the application of the three*
reserved colours RED, YELLOW and GREEN, and *for example for confirmation*. So
where a designer is unsure which coded colour applies, the rule of §4.2 directs
that BLUE or WHITE be used instead of risking a wrong reserved colour.

WHITE is frequently used in the **confirmation** mode (with BLUE — see
[[indicator-light-modes-of-use]], §4.1). It is also the colour of the
"double-confirmation" flashing sequence on illuminated buttons: a WHITE button may
flash to confirm a starting operation or transition has begun, then go steady to
confirm normal running ([[illuminated-push-button-modes-of-use]], §6.3, and
[[flashing-light]]). Gas-discharge lamps may replace WHITE where no confusion is
possible (§3.1.2 note 3). When an [[illuminated-push-button]] colour is hard to
assign, neutral WHITE is the fallback (§6.2). Note IEC 232 (nuclear) gives white
*other* meanings (preface). Contrast the reserved [[indicator-light-red]],
[[indicator-light-yellow]], [[indicator-light-green]] and the assignable
[[indicator-light-blue]]. The general signal-lamp symbol is [[08-10-01]] (colour
code **WH**).
