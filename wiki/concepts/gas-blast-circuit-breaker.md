# Gas-blast circuit-breaker

A gas-blast circuit-breaker is a [[circuit-breaker]] in which the arc is extinguished by a blast of gas ([[iec-56]] 3.102.9, IEV 441-14-30). The defining mechanism is a directed flow (blast) of gas across the arc that cools, lengthens and deionises it so that it cannot reignite at current zero. This is the generic parent category for blast-type interrupters: the [[air-blast-circuit-breaker]] (3.102.11) uses compressed air as the blast gas, while the [[sf6-circuit-breaker]] (3.102.10) uses sulphur hexafluoride. It is distinct from the [[oil-circuit-breaker]], [[vacuum-circuit-breaker]] and atmospheric [[air-circuit-breaker]]. Like all [[circuit-breaker]] designs it conducts continuous current through the [[main-contact]] of each [[pole]] and directs the blast onto the [[arcing-contact]] as the contacts part.
