# Encapsulation, embedding and potting

**Encapsulation** is the process of applying the [[encapsulation-compound|compound]]
to enclose any electrical device(s) by suitable means such as embedding or potting
(IEC 79-18 §3.5). It is the manufacturing act that realises
[[type-of-protection-m|encapsulation "m"]], and its production method must be
precisely documented and verified under §4.1.

The standard distinguishes the two means:

- **Embedding (§3.6)** — completely encasing the device(s) by **pouring the compound
  over them in a mould**, then **removing the device(s) from the mould** after the
  compound has solidified. The finished part is bare cured compound with no mould
  remaining.
- **Potting (§3.7)** — an embedding process in which **the mould remains attached**
  to the encased device(s). The retained housing may be metal or insulating material,
  which changes the [[thickness-of-compound|thickness requirements]]: §5.2.2 allows a
  1 mm compound layer behind a metal protective housing, and §5.2.3 permits no
  minimum compound layer behind an insulating housing ≥ 1 mm thick (the housing must
  then pass the [[non-specific-tests-encapsulation|annex C tests]]).

Whichever process is used, the result must satisfy [[free-space-and-voids|no-void]],
[[thickness-of-compound|thickness]], [[adhesion]] and [[external-connections-and-sealing|sealing]]
requirements.
