# Protective characteristics of an arrester

The **protective characteristics of an arrester** are the combination of ([[iec-99-1]] §48):

a) the **lightning-voltage impulse sparkover-voltage / time curve**, as determined in §61.3;
b) the **residual-voltage / discharge-current curve**, as determined in §62; and
c) for 10 000 A arresters rated 100 kV and higher, the **switching-voltage impulse sparkover-voltage /
   time curve**, as determined in §61.4.

Together these describe how well the arrester limits the voltage impressed on the protected apparatus:
the [[impulse-sparkover-voltage|sparkover curves]] give the voltage at which the
[[series-gap|gaps]] conduct as a function of surge speed, and the [[residual-voltage]] curve gives the
voltage the [[non-linear-series-resistor]] lets through at a given [[discharge-current]]. An arrester
having more favourable characteristics or lower protective levels than required is deemed to comply
(§54). Higher classes — 10 000 A first, then 5 000 A Series A — give the best protective levels (see
[[nominal-discharge-current]], [[arrester-application-guide]]).

In application (Appendix C / IEC 99-1A) these characteristics are condensed into the
[[impulse-protective-level]]: the maximum of the front-of-wave sparkover voltage ÷ 1.15, the 1.2/50
sparkover voltage, and the residual voltage at the discharge current. The protective level is then
compared with the insulation withstand strength of the protected equipment, requiring a minimum
**protective ratio of 1.2**. **Amended:** the protective-level/protective-ratio framing was introduced
by **Publication 99-1A (1962)** and folded into Appendix C of the 1970 second edition.
