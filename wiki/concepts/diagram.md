# Diagram

The most general document type defined by [[iec-113-1]]. Per §2.1, a **diagram**
may show the manner in which the various parts of a network, installation, group
of apparatus, or items of an apparatus are **interrelated and/or
interconnected**. It is the parent category from which the purpose-based classes
descend: an [[explanatory-diagram]] (study/understanding), a [[wiring-diagram]]
(making and checking connections), and a [[location-diagram-or-table]]
(where parts physically sit). A diagram conveys interrelation and
interconnection, distinguishing it from a [[chart-graph]], which instead shows
the interrelation between operations, time, physical quantities, or the states of
items.

IEC 113-1 classifies any actual diagram along two independent axes: by **purpose**
(§3–6) and by **method of representation** (§7–9 — number of conductors,
arrangement of symbols, topographical placing). The note to §1 makes the two
orthogonal: the *purpose* determines the type of diagram, while the *method(s)*
are chosen for convenience. A drawing may even satisfy several purposes at once,
yielding a [[mixed-diagram]] (Note to §3–6). The graphical symbols populating a
diagram come from the [[60617-1]] symbol family; IEC 113-1 governs how they are
assembled. A diagram may, where convenient, be replaced or supplemented by a
[[table-document]].
