# Power-frequency sparkover voltage of an arrester

The **power-frequency sparkover voltage of an arrester** is the value of the power-frequency voltage
— measured as the peak value divided by √2 — applied between the terminals of an arrester which
causes sparkover of all the [[series-gap|series gaps]] ([[iec-99-1]] §38). It expresses, as an
equivalent r.m.s. quantity, the power-frequency overvoltage level at which the arrester begins to
conduct, and is therefore the margin between the [[rated-voltage-arrester|rated voltage]] (which must
be sustained continuously) and the onset of operation. It is verified by the
[[power-frequency-sparkover-test]] (§60).

The principal requirement is that, for all classes except the 10 000 A heavy-duty class, **the
lowest power-frequency sparkover voltage of each sample tested shall be not less than 1.5 times the
[[rated-voltage-arrester|rated voltage]]** of the arrester (§60); for the 10 000 A heavy-duty class
the minimum is subject to agreement between manufacturer and purchaser. The **average** of five
applications is adopted as the power-frequency sparkover voltage used to compare an arrester before
and after other type tests — after the [[current-impulse-withstand-test]] and the
[[operating-duty-test]] the average value must not have changed by more than 10 %, which proves the
gaps and grading circuit have not been degraded. The dry power-frequency sparkover test is also the
minimum [[routine-and-acceptance-tests|routine test]] made on every arrester (§67).
