# Reignition

A reignition is a resumption of current between the contacts of a switching device during a breaking operation occurring less than a quarter of a cycle of power frequency after the most recent current zero ([[iec-56]] 3.105.46, IEV 441-17-45). The sub-quarter-cycle timing is precisely what distinguishes it from a [[restrike]], which occurs a quarter-cycle or more after current zero ([[iec-56]] 3.105.47). Because a reignition happens so soon after current zero, the [[recovery-voltage]] across the gap is still low, so it is generally far less onerous than a restrike and does not by itself cause the overvoltage escalation associated with late dielectric failures. Both phenomena describe the gap failing to hold off the rising [[recovery-voltage]] after arc extinction; the quarter-cycle boundary between them is the key classifying criterion for assessing interruption performance.
