# Outline of the ignition-temperature test method

The principle of the [[iec-79-4]] method (§3). A **known volume of the product to
be tested is injected into a heated open 200 ml [[erlenmeyer-test-flask|Erlenmeyer
flask]] containing air**. The contents of the flask are observed **in a darkened
room** until [[ignition-reaction|ignition]] occurs. The test is **repeated with
different flask temperatures and different sample volumes**. The **lowest flask
temperature at which ignition occurs** is taken to be the [[ignition-temperature]]
of the combustible in air at atmospheric pressure.

So the method is a **search over a two-dimensional matrix** — (flask temperature
× sample volume) — for the minimum igniting temperature. The flask is open (not
pressurised), the medium is ordinary air, and the energy source is purely
thermal (a hot flask wall), distinguishing this auto-ignition test from
spark-ignition tests such as [[iec-79-19]]'s references to intrinsic-safety
sparking.

The supporting apparatus that makes this outline repeatable is specified in §4:
the [[erlenmeyer-test-flask|test flask]] (4.1), the [[ignition-test-furnace|hot-air
furnace]] (4.2, detailed in Appendix A), [[ignition-test-thermocouples]] (4.3),
[[ignition-test-sample-introduction|syringes/pipettes/gas syringe]] (4.4), the
one-second timer (4.5) and the observation mirror (4.6). The step-by-step
execution is in [[ignition-test-procedure]] (§5), and what makes a run valid in
[[ignition-test-validity]] (§7).
