# Prospective peak current

The prospective peak current is the peak value of the first major loop of the [[prospective-current]] during the transient period following initiation ([[iec-56]] 3.105.3). The definition assumes the current is made by an ideal circuit-breaker — one with instantaneous and simultaneous transition of its pole impedance from infinity to zero across the terminals of each pole. The peak value may differ from one pole to another because it depends on the instant of current initiation relative to the voltage wave across each pole's terminals; the asymmetry of this first loop is what makes it a peak rather than the symmetrical r.m.s. value. It parallels the (peak) making current, which is the corresponding peak in an actual pole during a making operation ([[iec-56]] 3.105.8), and relates to the maximum prospective peak current of an a.c. circuit ([[iec-56]] 3.105.6).
