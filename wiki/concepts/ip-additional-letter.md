# IP additional letter (A, B, C, D)

An **optional** letter placed after the two characteristic numerals in the
[[ip-code-structure|IP Code]] ([[iec-529]] 2nd ed. clause 4), introduced by the
1989 edition. It indicates the **actual degree of protection of persons against
access to hazardous parts**, used when that protection is **higher** than the
level implied by the first characteristic numeral, or when **only** the
protection-against-access is being declared (first numeral replaced by X, e.g.
`IPXXC`, `IPX1C`). It does **not** describe object/dust ingress — that is the
first numeral's job.

The four letters correspond to the [[ip-access-probe|access probe]] that is kept
an adequate clearance from hazardous parts:

| Letter | Access probe | Protection against access with |
|--------|--------------|--------------------------------|
| **A** | sphere 50 mm diameter | back of hand |
| **B** | jointed test finger, 12 mm Ø × 80 mm | finger |
| **C** | test rod 2,5 mm Ø × 100 mm | tool |
| **D** | test wire 1,0 mm Ø × 100 mm | wire |

These mirror the access probes of [[ip-first-numeral-1]]–[[ip-first-numeral-4]],
but the additional letter lets a manufacturer claim, for example, finger-safety
("B") on an enclosure whose first numeral is only 2, or declare access-safety
alone where the object-ingress numeral is not specified. Examples from §9:
`IP20C`, `IP3XD`. The protection is verified with the corresponding access probe
under the [[ip-first-numeral-tests|first-numeral access tests]] (adequate
clearance maintained). The **1st edition (1976) had no additional letters** —
this concept is unique to the 2nd-edition IP Code.
