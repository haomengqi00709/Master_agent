# Meter symbol marking — scope and purpose

IEC 387's scope (§1) establishes how its symbols are used. The standard applies
to **letter and graphical symbols for a.c. electricity meters and their
auxiliary devices, independent of induction or static measurement elements** —
i.e. it covers both [[induction-meter]] and [[static-energy-meter]] types. **All
the symbols included in the standard may be marked on the name-plate, dial plate,
external labels or accessories of the meter** ([[meter-name-plate-and-dial]]).

The rationale is in the Introduction: the symbols **may be substituted for the
markings given in the different national languages**; they have the advantage of
**explaining the necessary technical characteristics irrespective of the country
for which the meter is intended.** This is what makes IEC 387 a marking standard
rather than a diagram-symbol dictionary like the [[60617-8]] parts — the symbols
go on the physical meter, not on circuit drawings.

The body of the standard organises these markings into nine symbol clauses plus a
normative annex: [[measuring-element-symbols]] (§4), [[meter-unit-symbols]] (§5),
[[measured-quantity-marking]] (§6), [[accuracy-class-and-protective-class]] (§7),
[[transformer-operated-meter-marking]] (§8), [[tariff-device-symbols]] (§9),
[[auxiliary-device-symbols]] (§10), [[moving-element-suspension-symbol]] (§11),
[[warning-symbol-meter]] (§12) and [[signal-port-symbols]] (Annex A).

See also: [[iec-387]] §1.
