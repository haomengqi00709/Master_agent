# Rated line-charging breaking current

Clause 4.107 of [[iec-56]] defines the rated line-charging breaking current as the maximum line-charging (capacitive) current the circuit-breaker shall be capable of breaking at its rated voltage under the prescribed conditions of use and behaviour, without exceeding the maximum permissible switching overvoltages specified by the manufacturer (suggested values in Table IX). This is the duty of switching off an unloaded overhead line, where the breaker interrupts the line's distributed charging current and a one-cosine [[recovery-voltage]] builds across the gap as the trapped charge holds the line side near peak voltage. Its specification is confined to three-pole breakers for switching three-phase overhead lines with rated voltage at or above 72.5 kV; standard values appear in Table VII (e.g. 10 A at 72.5 kV up to 500 A at 525 kV). A Table VII note ties the values to roughly 1.2 x U km of single-conductor line at 50 Hz. The chief concern is [[restrike]]; verified by the [[capacitive-current-switching-test]] ([[iec-56]] 4.107).
