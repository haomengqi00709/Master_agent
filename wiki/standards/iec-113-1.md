# IEC 113-1 — Diagrams, charts, tables: Definitions and classification

*Schémas, diagrammes, tableaux — Première partie: Définitions et classification.*
2nd edition, 1971 (a revision of Publication 113, 1959). Prepared by IEC Technical
Committee No. 3, *Graphical Symbols*.

A **documentation standard**, not a symbol dictionary. Where the [[60617-1]]
symbol family supplies the graphical symbols, IEC 113-1 sits one level up and
defines *what kinds of diagrams, charts and tables* those symbols are assembled
into, and how. TC-3 prepared it after observing that, having covered most of the
need for graphical symbols (see IEC Publication 117, *Recommended Graphical
Symbols*), the symbol publications should be supplemented with recommendations for
the preparation of diagrams. This Part 1 is the first of the IEC 113 series; the
series was to also include guiding principles for use and combination of symbols,
for preparation of diagrams, for supplementing/replacing diagrams by charts and
tables, and item designation (§Preface).

## Scope (clause 1)
The purpose is to **define and classify** diagrams, charts and tables used in
electrotechnology — documents made to explain functions, show connections, and
give information for manufacture, installation and maintenance. Two parallel
classifications are adopted: **(a) classification according to purpose**
([[explanatory-diagram]], [[explanatory-chart-or-table]], [[wiring-diagram]],
[[location-diagram-or-table]]); and **(b) classification according to method of
representation** (number of conductors, arrangement of symbols, topographical
placing). A note stresses these are orthogonal: in practice the *type* of diagram
is fixed by its **purpose**, while the **method(s) of representation** are chosen
for convenience and may be mixed on one drawing (§1, §Note to 7-9).

## Core definitions (§2)
- [[diagram]] — interrelation/interconnection of parts of a network or apparatus (§2.1)
- [[chart-graph]] — interrelation between operations, time, quantities or states (§2.2)
- [[table-document]] — replaces or supplements a diagram or chart (§2.2)

## Notable revisions from the 1959 edition (§Preface)
- Tables given more emphasis (and added to the title) as they increasingly replace
  diagrams and charts.
- Alternative terms dropped, e.g. *elementary diagram*, *terminal connection
  diagram*, *one-line representation*.
- Terms renamed, e.g. *représentation rassemblée → représentation assemblée*,
  *représentation semi-développée → représentation rangée*, *separated
  representation → detached representation*.
- Two concepts inserted: *location diagram or table* and *equivalent circuit
  diagram*.
