# Non-linear series resistor of an arrester

The **non-linear series resistor of an arrester** is the part of the lightning arrester which, by its
non-linear voltage–current characteristic, acts as a **low resistance to the flow of high discharge
currents** — thus limiting the voltage across the arrester terminals — and as a **high resistance at
normal power-frequency voltage** — thus limiting the magnitude of [[follow-current]] ([[iec-99-1]]
§6). This bidirectional, non-ohmic behaviour is the essence of the
[[non-linear-resistor-type-arrester|valve-type arrester]]: under a surge the resistor clamps the
[[residual-voltage]] (§37) appearing across the terminals during passage of the
[[discharge-current]] (§34); at power frequency, after the [[series-gap]] has resealed, its high
resistance throttles the follow current so the gap can interrupt it at a current zero.

The resistor's clamping performance is characterised by the **residual-voltage / discharge-current
curve**, measured in the [[residual-voltage-test]] (§62) with an 8/20 current impulse at
approximately 0.5, 1 and 2 times the [[nominal-discharge-current]]; the value at nominal current must
not exceed the maximum residual voltage in Table VII. The resistor elements alone are also the test
object for the long-duration current-impulse test on light-duty 10 000 A and 5 000 / 2 500 A
arresters (§63.3.3), where they are paralleled with other resistors and subjected to substantially
rectangular current impulses of the peak and duration given in Table IV. The resistor must show no
puncture or flashover after the [[current-impulse-withstand-test]] (§63.2).
