# Rated voltage (U_r)

The rated voltage of a circuit-breaker is the highest r.m.s. phase-to-phase voltage at which it is designed to operate, defining the upper limit of service voltage. For HV AC circuit-breakers, sub-clause 4.1 of IEC Publication 694 applies directly ([[iec-56]] 4.1). Standard rated voltages follow internationally agreed series (Series I, based on general practice, and Series II, based on North-American 60 Hz practice). For example, the rated voltage 27 kV is also used in the United States and Canada. The rated voltage is the reference quantity that determines the power-frequency recovery voltage in breaking duties and fixes the [[rated-insulation-level]] (4.2), the standard values of [[transient-recovery-voltage]] (4.102), and the conditions defining the [[rated-short-circuit-breaking-current]] (4.101).

The standard value lists (from the earlier Part 2, [[iec-56]] / IEC 56-2 §2, carried into clause 4): for rated voltages **72.5 kV and below** — Series I: 3.6; 7.2; 12; 17.5; 24; 36; 52; 72.5 kV; Series II: 4.76; 8.25; 15.0; 15.5; 25.8; 38; 48.3; 72.5 kV. For rated voltages **above 72.5 kV**: 100; 123; 145; 170; 245; 300; 362; 420; 525; 765 kV (in North-American practice 550 kV is preferred to 525 kV). See [[iec-56-2-rated-characteristics]].
