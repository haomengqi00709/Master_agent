# Sample introduction (syringes, pipettes, gas syringe)

The apparatus for introducing a **known, measured volume** of sample into the
[[erlenmeyer-test-flask|test flask]] ([[iec-79-4]] §4.4) — accurate volume
delivery matters because sample volume is one axis of the test matrix and one of
the [[factors-affecting-ignition-temperature|factors affecting the result]].

**Liquid samples** are introduced by **either**:
- **a)** a **0.25 ml or 1 ml hypodermic syringe** with a stainless-steel needle of
  **0.15 mm (0.006 in) maximum bore diameter**, calibrated in units not greater
  than **0.01 ml**; or
- **b)** a **calibrated 1 ml pipette** that discharges 1 ml of distilled water at
  room temperature in **35–40 droplets**.

**Gaseous samples** are introduced by a **200 ml gas-tight calibrated glass
syringe fitted with a three-way stopcock and connecting tubes**.

> **Note (§4.4):** Precaution against **flash-back** should be taken. One method is
> illustrated in **Figure 6** — a gas-injection arrangement with a flame trap
> (sintered glass disks ~10 × 3 mm), an antechamber, a 1 mm plastic-foil flame
> trap and an inflatable rubber reservoir feeding the gas-tight syringe.

How these are used is specified in [[ignition-test-procedure|the procedure]]
(§5.2): liquids are injected as **droplets into the centre** of the flask as
quickly as possible (completed in **2 s**), then the syringe/pipette is quickly
withdrawn, taking care **not to wet the walls**; gases are injected at about
**25 ml/s** after flushing the syringe and tubes. Suitable **initial** volumes
(§5.2.3) are **0.07 ml** (liquid) and **20 ml** (gaseous).
