# Short-line fault test

Short-line fault tests are short-circuit tests additional to [[iec-56]] 6.106, applying only to three-pole breakers for direct connection to overhead lines, rated 52 kV and above with rated short-circuit breaking current exceeding 12.5 kA ([[iec-56]] 6.109.1). They are onerous because a fault a short distance down the line imposes a steep, sawtooth (approximately triangular) [[transient-recovery-voltage]] on the line side — the line-side oscillation reaching peak ui in time ti — superimposed on the source-side recovery, severely stressing the gap just after current zero. Source impedance corresponds to about 100% rated current; two line impedances reduce the a.c. component to 90% and 75% ([[iec-56]] 6.109.2). The single-phase circuit combines supply and line-side components ([[iec-56]] 6.109.3-6.109.4). The [[test-duty]] comprises opening-only operating sequences at (90 plus/minus 5)% and (75 plus/minus 5)% with the appropriate prospective TRV ([[iec-56]] 6.109.5). See [[short-line-fault]].
