# Use of colours

**IEC 73 §3.1.** Because a specific meaning or significance is assigned to each
colour, the different colours **must be easily distinguishable from each other**.
For this reason [[iec-73]] deals only with a restricted palette:

- **§3.1.1 — Indicator lights (transmitted light):** RED, YELLOW, GREEN, BLUE and
  WHITE, each usable with either a clear lens or with diffused light. See
  [[indicator-light]].
- **§3.1.2 — Push-buttons (surface colours, in incident white light):** RED,
  YELLOW, GREEN, BLUE, BLACK, WHITE and GREY. See [[push-button]].

Several notes refine this: the colour YELLOW may shade towards orange and is
sometimes called *amber* or *orange* (note 1); the exact colorimetric definitions
are given by **ISO 3864** (note 2); gas-discharge lamps may be used in place of
WHITE where no confusion is possible (note 3); and it is **not necessary to use
all** the specified colours — an appropriate selection may be made in each case
(note 4). This restricted, distinguishable palette is what makes the coded
meanings (Table I for lights, Table II for buttons) reliable, and it underpins
[[choice-of-colours]] and [[permanence-of-colours]].
