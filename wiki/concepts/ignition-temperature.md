# Ignition temperature

The **lowest temperature at which ignition occurs when the method prescribed in
the standard is followed** ([[iec-79-4]] §2.1). Operationally (§6), it is the
lowest **flask temperature** at which a sample of the chemically pure vapour or
gas in air, at atmospheric pressure, produces [[ignition-reaction|ignition]]
within the 5-minute [[ignition-lag]] limit during the tests of §5 — recorded
only if the result satisfies the [[ignition-test-validity|validity requirements]]
of §7. The corresponding ignition lag and barometric pressure are recorded
alongside it (§6).

It is a **property of the substance under this specific test geometry**, not an
absolute physical constant: the value depends on the heated open 200 ml
[[erlenmeyer-test-flask|Erlenmeyer flask]], the injection method, and the
[[factors-affecting-ignition-temperature|factors affecting the result]]. This is
why §3 carefully fixes the apparatus and procedure. Because the test reports the
*lowest* igniting flask temperature found over a matrix of temperatures and
sample volumes (§5.4, refined in 2 °C steps), the number is a conservative
"auto-ignition" figure for Ex-safety use.

The measured value is the input to **IEC 79-8** classification of maximum surface
temperatures and hence to the [[temperature-classes-t1-t6|temperature classes
T1–T6]] that limit the surface temperature of Ex apparatus. Reference results
are tabulated in [[ignition-temperature-data-list]] (folded from amendment
[[iec-79-4a]]); calibration values appear in Table I (§4.2): n-heptane 220 °C,
ethylene 435 °C, benzene 560 °C.
