# Name-plate and dial of a meter

IEC 387 defines the two marked surfaces of a meter (§3.17–3.18):

- **Name-plate of a meter** (§3.17): *easily readable plate, placed either inside
  or outside the meter, which states in particular the electrical quantities
  corresponding to the conditions of use and may also include the symbols.*
- **Dial** (§3.18, after IEV 301-07-04): *part of an indicating device carrying
  the scale or scales and generally including other information to characterize
  the instrument.* A NOTE adds that in some cases the name-plate and the dial may
  be combined.

These surfaces are where almost every IEC 387 symbol lives: §1 states the symbols
"may be marked on the name-plate, dial plate, external labels or accessories." In
particular the unit symbol for the measured quantity must be marked
**conspicuously on the dial** (§6, [[measured-quantity-marking]]); the accuracy
class, [[meter-constant]] and protective class go on the name-plate or dial
([[accuracy-class-and-protective-class]], §7); and the transformer ratios *taken
into account* by the register are marked on the name-plate or dial, while those
*not accounted for* go on a **supplementary plate** on the cover
([[transformer-operated-meter-marking]], §8). The supplementary plate is a
recurring third surface used for changeable values (excess-power thresholds,
multiplying factors).
