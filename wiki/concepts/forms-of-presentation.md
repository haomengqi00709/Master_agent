# Forms of presentation of information (IEC 1082-1 §2.1.2)

One of the two axes by which every [[medium-and-document|document]] is named (the
other being the *type* of information). IEC 1082-1 §2.1.2 enumerates the seven
**forms in which information may be presented**:

1. **Pictorial form (§2.1.2.1)** — graphical presentation depicting the shape,
   size, etc. of a physical part or assembly, often to scale.
2. **Plan (§2.1.2.2)** — a drawing showing a horizontal view, section or cut.
3. **Diagram (§2.1.2.3)** — graphical presentation that, *by the use of graphical
   symbols and outlines with inscriptions*, depicts the **relations among the
   components and parts of a system or equipment, including the interconnections**.
   This is the form populated by [[60617-3|IEC 617]] symbols and elaborated by
   Parts 2 and 3. (Compare the purpose-based [[diagram]] of the older [[iec-113-1]].)
4. **Map (§2.1.2.4)** — graphical presentation of an installation with respect to
   its surrounding topography.
5. **Chart, graph (§2.1.2.5)** — graphical presentation describing the *behaviour*
   of a system, e.g. relations between two or more variable quantities, operations
   or states (cf. the time-sequence chart, Part 1 figure 21). See [[chart-graph]].
6. **Table, list (§2.1.2.6)** — presentation form using **columns and rows**.
   A footnote records that IEC 1082 makes **no difference between the terms *table*
   and *list*** — used interchangeably (echoed in Part 3 §1.1).
7. **Textual form (§2.1.2.7)** — presentation using text, e.g. written
   instructions and descriptions.

A footnote flags that *plan, diagram, map, chart, table* each carry **two
meanings**: the *form of presentation* (as defined here) and the *document* itself.
The same subject matter may be rendered in several of these forms — which is why
most document classes have both a diagram and a table variant (see
[[document-classification-iec1082]], [[table-document]]).
