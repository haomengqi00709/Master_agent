# Non-self-restoring insulation

Non-self-restoring insulation is insulation which loses its insulating properties, or does not recover them completely, after a disruptive discharge ([[iec-56]] 3.101.23). A disruptive discharge completely bridges the insulation under electric stress, reducing the inter-electrode voltage to near zero ([[iec-56]] 3.101.24). In a solid dielectric a disruptive discharge — termed a puncture — produces permanent loss of dielectric strength, so solid internal insulation is generally non-self-restoring ([[iec-56]] 3.101.24, Note 2). This is the direct opposite of [[self-restoring-insulation]], which fully recovers. It is characteristic of much [[internal-insulation]], particularly solid parts shielded from external conditions, whereas [[external-insulation]] in air is usually self-restoring. Because each disruptive discharge is potentially destructive, withstand verification toward the [[rated-insulation-level]] uses non-destructive withstand tests rather than repeated breakdown.
