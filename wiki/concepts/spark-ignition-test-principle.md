# Spark-ignition test — principle

The principle of the test ([[iec-79-3]] §4.1). The circuit to be tested is
connected to the **contacts of the spark-test apparatus**, which sit inside an
[[explosion-chamber]] filled with the appropriate
[[explosive-test-mixture|explosive test mixture]] (chosen per **IEC 79-11
sub-clause 9.1.2** for the apparatus group). In accordance with IEC 79-11, the
**parameters of the circuit are adjusted to achieve a prescribed safety factor**,
and a test is then made to determine whether or not **ignition of the explosive
test mixture takes place within a defined number of operations** of the
[[contact-mechanism]].

So the test has three coupled ingredients: (1) a worst-case spark source — the
rotating [[tungsten-wire-electrode]] sliding over the slotted
[[cadmium-disc-electrode]], producing make- and break-sparks; (2) a known gas
mixture in the chamber; and (3) a circuit whose energy has been raised by the
safety factor above its rated condition (see
[[safety-factor-and-ignition-criterion]]). A circuit passes — is judged
[[intrinsically-safe-circuit|intrinsically safe]] — if no ignition occurs in the
prescribed number of operations. Before and after each series of tests the
apparatus sensitivity is verified by [[spark-test-calibration]].
