# High current impulse of an arrester (§2.31)

The high current impulse is **the peak value of [[discharge-current]] having a 4/10
impulse shape which is used to test the stability of the arrester on direct
lightning strokes** ([[iec-99-4]] §2.31). The 4/10 designation (see
[[impulse-waveshape-terminology]]) gives a very high, short, energetic surge
representing a near or direct strike to the line.

The high current impulse is applied in the **high-current-impulse
[[operating-duty-test|operating duty test]]** (§7.5.4) — which covers 1 500 A,
2 500 A, 5 000 A and 10 000 A [[line-discharge-class|class 1]] arresters and
[[high-lightning-duty-arrester|High Lightning Duty]] arresters. The section is
subjected to **two** high current impulses (4/10) with peak per Table 6:

| Arrester class | High-current peak, 4/10 (kA) |
|----------------|------------------------------|
| 10 000 A | 100 |
| 5 000 A | 65 |
| 2 500 A | 25 |
| 1 500 A | 10 |

Between the two impulses the section is preheated to 60 °C ±3 °C; the wave
tolerances are 90–110 % of peak, 3,5–4,5 µs front, 9–17 µs half-value (§7.5.4.2).
High Lightning Duty arresters instead take **three 30/80 impulses at 40 kA**
(Annex C). In the switching-surge duty test the second conditioning step is **two
100 kA 4/10** high current impulses (§7.5.5.1). After the impulses, power-frequency
voltage is applied to prove [[thermal-stability]].
