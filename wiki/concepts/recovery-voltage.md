# Recovery voltage

The recovery voltage is the voltage that appears across the terminals of a pole of a circuit-breaker after the breaking of the current ([[iec-56]] 3.105.23, IEV 441-17-25). It is conventionally considered in two successive intervals: an initial transient period, during which the [[transient-recovery-voltage]] (TRV) may exist, followed by a second period during which only the power-frequency recovery voltage exists ([[iec-56]] 3.105.24, 3.105.26). This voltage is what stresses the contact gap immediately after arc extinction at current zero; if it rises faster than the gap regains dielectric strength, the result is a [[reignition]] (within a quarter-cycle of current zero) or a [[restrike]] (a quarter-cycle or more after current zero). The rated transient recovery voltage for terminal faults is one of the circuit-breaker's defining rated characteristics ([[iec-56]] clause 4).
