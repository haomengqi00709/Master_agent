# Earth fault factor

The earth fault factor, at a selected location of a three-phase system (generally the point of installation of equipment) and for a given system configuration, is the ratio of the highest r.m.s. phase-to-earth power-frequency voltage on a sound phase during a fault to earth (affecting one or more phases at any point) to the r.m.s. phase-to-earth power-frequency voltage that would be obtained at the same location without the fault ([[iec-56]] 3.101.8). It is a pure numerical ratio greater than 1 characterizing the earthing conditions of the system as viewed from that location, independent of actual operating voltage. It equals the square root of 3 times the older factor of earthing, and is computed from the phase-sequence impedance components using subtransient reactances for rotating machines. It will not exceed 1.4 in an effectively [[earthed-neutral-system]], but approaches the square root of 3 in an [[isolated-neutral-system]] or [[resonant-earthed-system]]; this factor sets the sound-phase overvoltage that determines the required [[rated-insulation-level]].
