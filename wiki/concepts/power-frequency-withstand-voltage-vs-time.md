# Power-frequency voltage versus time characteristic (§2.37, §5.10)

The power-frequency (withstand) voltage versus time characteristic of an arrester
**shows the maximum time durations for which corresponding power-frequency voltages
may be applied to the arrester without causing damage or thermal instability, under
specified conditions** ([[iec-99-4]] §2.37, in accordance with §5.10). It expresses
the arrester's tolerance of **temporary overvoltages (TOV)**: the higher the
applied power-frequency voltage, the shorter the time before [[thermal-runaway]]
threatens.

The requirement (§5.10) is that the manufacturer supply data on the allowable time
duration of power-frequency voltage — and the corresponding voltage — that may be
applied **after the arrester has been preheated to 60 °C and subjected to the high
current or [[line-discharge-class]] energy duty**, without damage or thermal
runaway. The information is presented as power-frequency-voltage-versus-time curves
with the prior impulse energy consumption stated on the curve. Such curves are
needed to select the arrester [[arrester-rated-voltage|rated voltage]] for local
lightning/switching/TOV conditions; they may be established by calculation. The
curve should cover **0,1 s to 20 min**, extended to **24 h** for isolated-neutral
or resonant-earthed systems without earth-fault clearing (notes to §5.10). If
experimental verification is agreed, the procedure of **Annex D** is used (the last
part of the operating-duty sequence with modified voltage level/duration, three
points sufficient). Compare the temporary-overvoltage capability captured here with
[[continuous-operating-voltage]] (continuous) and [[arrester-rated-voltage]] (the
10 s TOV rating).
