# Rated short-circuit making current

The rated short-circuit making current is the peak current the circuit-breaker can close onto at its [[rated-voltage]] without damage, accounting for asymmetry at the instant of closing (see Figure 8). It shall be 2.5 times the r.m.s. value of the a.c. component of the [[rated-short-circuit-breaking-current]] for 50 Hz, and 2.6 times for 60 Hz ([[iec-56]] 4.103). The factor reflects the maximum asymmetrical peak arising from the d.c. offset at a 45 ms time constant and the chosen [[rated-frequency]]. The rated short-circuit making current is equal to the [[rated-peak-withstand-current]] (4.6), i.e. the closed-position electrodynamic withstand. It is demonstrated in short-circuit making and breaking tests performed according to the [[rated-operating-sequence]] (4.104).

**Amended (1992, Amd. 1):** [[iec-56-amendment-1]] rewrites the test clause 6.104.2 — making ability is proven in test-duty No. 4, requiring closing both against a symmetrical current (= [[rated-short-circuit-breaking-current]]) and against a fully asymmetrical current (= rated making current), with reduced-voltage CO operations allowed where pre-arcing prevents reaching the rated making current.
