# Rated voltage of an arrester

The **rated voltage of an arrester** is the designated maximum permissible r.m.s. value of
power-frequency voltage between its terminals at which it is designed to operate correctly; this
voltage may be applied to the arrester continuously without changing its operating characteristics
([[iec-99-1]] §10). It is the reference quantity for the whole device: normal service requires that
the power-frequency voltage between line and earth terminals does not exceed it (§2), and selecting
the rating at least equal to the highest phase-to-earth voltage is what assures extinction of the
[[follow-current]] and proper reseal of the [[series-gap]] (see [[arrester-application-guide]]). The
rating must appear on the [[arrester-identification|rating plate]] (§50) and shall be divisible by 6.

**Standard voltage ratings** (kV r.m.s., §51) range from 0.175 kV up to 198 kV, including
0.175, 0.280, 0.500, 0.660, 3, 4.5, 6, 7.5, 9, 10.5, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30, 33, 36, 39, 42,
54, 60, 75, 84, 96, 102, 108, 120, 126, 138, 156, 174, 186 and 198 kV. For ratings above 198 kV the
arrester rating is determined as directed in Appendix C ([[arrester-application-guide]]). The rated
voltage scales the requirements: the [[power-frequency-sparkover-voltage]] must be at least 1.5 times
the rated voltage (§60); the maximum [[impulse-sparkover-voltage|impulse-sparkover]] and
[[residual-voltage]] limits are tabulated against rated voltage (Tables VI and VII); and performance
for other ratings of the same design within ±25 % (or 6 kV, whichever is greater) of a test sample may
be inferred by proportioning (§60, §61). Standard rated frequencies are given by
[[rated-frequency-arrester]].
