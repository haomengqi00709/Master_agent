# Prospective current

The prospective current (of a circuit, with respect to a circuit-breaker) is the current that would flow in the circuit if each pole of the circuit-breaker were replaced by a conductor of negligible impedance ([[iec-56]] 3.105.2, IEV 441-17-01). It therefore represents the current the circuit itself can deliver, determined by the source and circuit impedances alone and independent of the limiting effect of the actual breaker. It is the reference quantity from which several rated and test quantities are derived: the [[prospective-peak-current]] is the peak value of its first major loop during the transient period following initiation ([[iec-56]] 3.105.3), and the prospective breaking current is the prospective current evaluated at the instant the arc is initiated during breaking ([[iec-56]] 3.105.9). Related notions include prospective symmetrical current and prospective making current ([[iec-56]] 3.105.5, 3.105.7).

**In [[iec-99-4]] (§2.38):** the surge-arrester standard defines prospective current of a circuit
identically in substance — *the current which would flow at a given location in a circuit if it were
short-circuited at that location by a link of negligible impedance.* It is the reference quantity for
the short-circuit/pressure-relief context of arresters (see [[pressure-relief]]).
