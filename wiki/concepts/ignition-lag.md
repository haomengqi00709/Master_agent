# Ignition lag

The **period which elapses between the instant of completed injection of the
sample and ignition** ([[iec-79-4]] §2.3). It is the induction (delay) time of
the auto-ignition reaction inside the heated [[erlenmeyer-test-flask|test
flask]], measured with a timer calibrated in **one-second intervals** (§4.5).

The ignition lag is central to the method in two ways:

- **It bounds the definition of [[ignition-reaction|ignition]]** — a reaction
  counts only if its lag does **not exceed 5 min** (§2.2). A slow reaction
  appearing later is disregarded.
- **It is measured and recorded on every test** — in the procedure (§5.3) the
  timer is *started as soon as the sample has been completely injected* and
  *stopped immediately a flame is observed*; the temperature and ignition lag are
  recorded. If no flame appears, the timer is stopped after 5 min and the test
  discontinued.

At the reported [[ignition-temperature]] the corresponding ignition lag is
recorded with the result and the barometric pressure (§6), and it forms part of
the permanent data record (§8). In practice the lag lengthens as the flask
temperature approaches the ignition temperature from above, so the lag is a
useful diagnostic that the test is near the true minimum.
