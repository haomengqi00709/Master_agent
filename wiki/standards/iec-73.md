# IEC 73 — Colours of indicator lights and push-buttons

*Couleurs des voyants lumineux de signalisation et des boutons-poussoirs.*
2nd edition, 1975. Prepared by **IEC Technical Committee No. 16** (Terminal
Markings and Other Identifications).

A **convention / requirements standard** (not a symbol dictionary): it assigns a
fixed safety/state meaning to each colour used in indicator lights and
push-buttons, and a convention for choosing them. Distilled here per the
LLM-Wiki method — each general requirement, each coded colour, each push-button
function and the illuminated-push-button rules each get their own concept page,
cross-linked to the [[60617-8]] signalling-lamp symbols and [[60617-7]]
push-button symbols.

## Scope & object (clause 1)
Specifies colours and establishes a convention for their use in [[indicator-light]]s
and for [[push-button]]s wherever such devices are employed (§1.1) — from a single
light/button up to complex control desks. The **object** (§1.2) is to increase
safety of personnel and facilitate correct operation/maintenance by giving
uniformity to the significance of certain colours. Colours alone are often
insufficient, so they should be supplemented by symbols (see **IEC 417**) or
written information (§1.3). Of general application across all fields of electrical
engineering; existing well-established codes may continue where incorrect
operation cannot cause danger (e.g. some telecom/recording equipment).

## History (preface)
Replaces **Part 2 of IEC 54** (indicating lamps for circuit-breakers) and the
**first edition of IEC 73, 1955** (colour of push-buttons). TC 16 set up a working
group in London, September 1968, to harmonize colour use across all of electrical
engineering; drafts were discussed in Baden-Baden (1970), Zagreb (1971) and Paris
(1973). Has wider application than Clause 8 of **IEC 204-1** (electrical equipment
of machine-tools) and deviates slightly from Appendix B of **IEC 232** (nuclear
reactor instrumentation, where green and white have other meanings). Takes account
of **ISO/R 408 → ISO 3864** (safety colours). The Danish National Committee did not
accept the text.

## Definitions (clause 2)
[[indicator-light]] (§2.1) · [[push-button]] (§2.2) · [[illuminated-push-button]] (§2.3).
For the purposes of this standard, **BLACK, WHITE and GREY are regarded as
colours**. Further definitions: see the I.E.V. (IEC 50); push-button per I.E.V.
15-15-140.

## General requirements (clause 3)
[[use-of-colours]] (§3.1) · [[choice-of-colours]] (§3.2) · [[permanence-of-colours]] (§3.3) · [[flashing-light]] (§3.4).
The light colours are **RED, YELLOW, GREEN, BLUE, WHITE** (§3.1.1); push-button
surface colours add **BLACK and GREY** (§3.1.2).

## Indicator lights (clause 4)
[[indicator-light-modes-of-use]] (§4.1 — indication vs confirmation).
Table I (§4.2) — the colour code; RED, YELLOW and GREEN have **reserved**

- [[indicator-light-yellow]] — caution
- [[indicator-light-green]] — safety
- [[indicator-light-blue]] — specific meaning as needed
- [[indicator-light-white]] — no specific meaning (neutral)

## Note on the OCR sources (73 vs 73-1)
Two OCR files exist. `iec-73.txt` is the full document (cover → Appendix A).
`iec-73-1.txt` is **not a separate amendment**: it is a partial re-scan of the
**same** Second edition (1975) — only the cover, clause 4 with Table I, and Table
II were captured, and their text is identical to the corresponding clauses in
`iec-73.txt`. There is no distinct technical content in 73-1; it is folded in here
with no `**Amended:**` notes required.
