# Self-restoring insulation

Self-restoring insulation is insulation which completely recovers its insulating properties after a disruptive discharge ([[iec-56]] 3.101.22). A disruptive discharge is a phenomenon associated with failure of insulation under electric stress in which the discharge completely bridges the insulation and reduces the voltage between electrodes to zero or nearly zero ([[iec-56]] 3.101.24). In liquid or gaseous dielectrics the loss of dielectric strength may be only temporary, so such media are typically self-restoring; this is why much [[external-insulation]] (air clearances, where discharges appear as flashover or sparkover) behaves this way. It contrasts directly with [[non-self-restoring-insulation]], which does not fully recover. The distinction matters for testing philosophy: self-restoring insulation tolerates repeated disruptive-discharge tests, and its withstand contributes to the [[rated-insulation-level]].
