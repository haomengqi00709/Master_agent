# Rated small inductive breaking current

In [[iec-56]] this is clause 4.112, Rated small inductive breaking current, which the 1987 standard records only as "Under consideration" — no standard values are assigned. Conceptually it is the maximum small inductive current (e.g. the magnetizing current of unloaded transformers and reactors, or shunt-reactor current) that the circuit-breaker shall be capable of breaking at its rated voltage under the prescribed conditions. The defining hazard is current chopping: interrupting a small, lagging inductive current before its natural zero traps magnetic energy that converts to a switching overvoltage on the load side. Because no rated values are tabulated, the duty is demonstrated by the [[magnetizing-and-small-inductive-current-switching-test]] (clause 6.112). It is one of the optional rated quantities listed in the nameplate clause, distinct from capacitive switching ([[rated-line-charging-breaking-current]], [[rated-single-capacitor-bank-breaking-current]]) and from [[rated-short-circuit-breaking-current]] ([[iec-56]] 4.112).
