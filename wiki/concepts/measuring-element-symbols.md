# Symbols for the measuring element (clause 4)

IEC 387 §4 and **Table 1** define schematic symbols showing how a meter's
measuring elements are connected, given as examples. The drawing convention
(§4) is precise:

- **Each voltage circuit is represented by a line**; **each current circuit by a
  small circle.**
- At the end of each line (voltage circuit), a circle (current circuit) is placed
  to have a **point of common connection** with that voltage circuit.
- If a current and a voltage circuit with such a common point are **not part of
  the same electro-magnet**, the current circle is joined to the **mid-point** of
  the voltage line by a guide line **not more than half the thickness** of the
  first line.
- If an electro-magnet carries two current circuits whose turns are in the ratio
  **1/k**, the diameters of the representative circles are in approximately the
  same ratio.
- The **angle between two lines** represents the **phase angle** between the
  corresponding voltages (positive direction toward the common point in two-line
  symbols 4.9/4.10; trigonometrical direction for triangular symbol 4.8).
- A current under a **positive** voltage direction is a **black (filled) circle**;
  under a **negative** direction, a **white (open) circle**.

Table 1 enumerates arrangements 4.1–4.10 for **watt-hour or var-hour meters**:
4.1 one element / one current + one voltage circuit (1-phase 2-wire); 4.2 one
voltage + two current circuits; 4.3 two elements for 1-phase 3-wire; 4.4 two
elements on a three-phase circuit (neutral-to-phase voltages); 4.5 two elements,
**2-wattmeter method** (3-phase 3-wire); 4.6 three elements, **3-wattmeter
method** (3-phase 4-wire); 4.7 two elements for a two-phase 3-wire circuit; and
the dedicated **var-hour** symbols 4.8 (three elements, corresponds to figure 1),
4.9 (two elements with current circuits of n and 2n turns, ratio 1:2, figure 2)
and 4.10 (two elements, figure 3) — 4.8 applies to 3-phase 3/4-wire, 4.9 and 4.10
to 3-phase 3-wire.
