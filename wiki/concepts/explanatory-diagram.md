# Explanatory diagram

The first purpose-class of [[diagram]] in [[iec-113-1]] (§3). **Explanatory
diagrams are intended to facilitate the study and understanding of the
functioning** of an installation or equipment. They sit alongside the other
purpose classes — [[explanatory-chart-or-table]] (§4), [[wiring-diagram]] (§5) and
[[location-diagram-or-table]] (§6) — and are distinguished by their aim of
explaining *function* rather than guiding the making of connections or recording
physical location.

IEC 113-1 defines **three types** of explanatory diagram (§3):

- [[block-diagram]] (§3.1) — a relatively simple diagram giving the principle of
  operation, using block symbols or pictures without necessarily showing all
  connections.
- [[circuit-diagram]] (§3.2) — the detailed functional diagram showing electrical
  connections and other links by symbols (U.S.A.: *schematic diagram*).
- [[equivalent-circuit-diagram]] (§3.3) — a special circuit diagram for the
  analysis and calculation of circuit characteristics.

An explanatory diagram may share a drawing with a wiring diagram: the same drawing
may form both an explanatory and a wiring diagram, producing a [[mixed-diagram]]
(Note to §3–6). The explanatory chart/table family ([[sequence-chart-or-table]],
[[time-sequence-chart-or-table]]) exists to *facilitate the study of these
diagrams* and to give additional information (§4).
