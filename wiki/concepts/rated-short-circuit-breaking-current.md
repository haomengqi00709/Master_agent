# Rated short-circuit breaking current

The rated short-circuit breaking current is the highest short-circuit current the circuit-breaker can break under prescribed conditions, in a circuit whose power-frequency recovery voltage corresponds to its [[rated-voltage]] and whose [[transient-recovery-voltage]] equals the rated value of 4.102. For three-pole circuit-breakers the a.c. component relates to a three-phase short-circuit ([[iec-56]] 4.101). It is characterised by two values: the r.m.s. value of its a.c. component (the rated short-circuit current) and the percentage d.c. component (which may be ignored if it does not exceed 20%). The a.c. component is selected from the R 10 series:
- 6.3, 8, 10, 12.5, 16, 20, 25, 31.5, 40, 50, 63, 80, 100 kA (4.101.1).
The d.c. component corresponds to time interval t = minimum opening time (plus a half-cycle for auxiliary-power tripping), per Figure 9 (45 ms time constant). It equals the [[rated-short-time-withstand-current]] (4.5).
