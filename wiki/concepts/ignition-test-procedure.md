# Procedure (§5)

The step-by-step execution of the [[iec-79-4]] ignition-temperature test.

**5.1 Furnace temperature.** First adjust the [[ignition-test-furnace|furnace]] so
the [[erlenmeyer-test-flask|flask]] reaches the desired **uniform** temperature.

**5.2 Sample injection.** When testing samples whose boiling point is at or near
room temperature, keep the injection system at a temperature that prevents any
change of state before the sample enters the flask.
- **5.2.1 Liquid samples** — inject the required volume as **droplets into the
  centre** of the flask with the hypodermic syringe or pipette, as quickly as
  possible so the operation is **completed in 2 s**; withdraw quickly; **avoid
  wetting the walls**.
- **5.2.2 Gaseous samples** — fill the gas-tight syringe and tubes, flushing
  repeatedly to be sure the system is completely filled with the test gas; inject
  the required volume at about **25 ml/s**, as constant as possible; withdraw the
  filling tube quickly.
- **5.2.3 Initial sample volume** — suitable starting volumes are **0.07 ml**
  (liquid) and **20 ml** (gaseous).

**5.3 Observations.** Start the timer as soon as injection is complete; stop it
immediately a flame is observed; record temperature and [[ignition-lag]]. If no
flame appears, stop the timer after **5 min** and discontinue the test (the
[[ignition-reaction|ignition]] definition's lag limit).

**5.4 Subsequent tests.** Repeat at different temperatures and sample volumes
until the **minimum [[ignition-temperature]] is obtained**. Between tests flush
the flask completely with clean dry air and allow time for the flask temperature
to re-stabilise before the next injection. The **final tests are made in
temperature steps of 2 °C** until the lowest igniting temperature is found.

**5.5 Confirmatory tests.** Repeat the final series of tests **five times**.

The lowest temperature giving ignition across §5, if it passes
[[ignition-test-validity|§7 validity]], is recorded as the ignition temperature
(§6) with its ignition lag and the barometric pressure.
