# Ignition (the ignition event)

In [[iec-79-4]] §2.2, **ignition** is defined as *a reaction in the test flask
which is evidenced by a clearly perceptible flame and/or explosion, and for which
the [[ignition-lag|ignition lag]] does not exceed 5 min*. Three things make a
reaction count as ignition under this method:

1. **Location** — the reaction must occur inside the heated 200 ml
   [[erlenmeyer-test-flask|Erlenmeyer flask]].
2. **Evidence** — a *clearly perceptible flame and/or explosion*. Observation is
   made in a **darkened room** (§3) so that even a faint flame is visible; an
   observation mirror is recommended ~250 mm above the flask (§4.6) to view the
   interior conveniently.
3. **Timing** — the [[ignition-lag]] (time from completed injection to ignition)
   must be **≤ 5 min**. A reaction appearing after 5 min does not count: in the
   procedure (§5.3) the timer is stopped and the test discontinued if no flame is
   seen within 5 minutes.

This strict, observable definition is what makes the [[ignition-temperature]]
reproducible between laboratories. A "no-ignition" result at a given flask
temperature drives the test downward/upward in temperature and sample volume
(§5.4) until the lowest igniting temperature is bracketed in 2 °C steps.
