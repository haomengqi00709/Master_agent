# Continuous current of an arrester (§2.33)

The continuous current is **the current flowing through the arrester when energized
at the [[continuous-operating-voltage]]** (U_c) ([[iec-99-4]] §2.33). Since a
[[metal-oxide-surge-arrester]] has no series gap, a small current flows
continuously through the [[metal-oxide-resistor|metal-oxide resistors]] at U_c —
this is the standing current the resistors must tolerate without overheating.

The continuous current consists of a **resistive** and a **capacitive** component
and may vary with temperature, stray capacitance and external pollution; the value
measured on a test sample may therefore differ from that of a complete arrester
(note to §2.33). For comparison it is expressed by either its r.m.s. or peak value.
It is the **resistive** component that is significant for thermal behaviour: its
peak is monitored (alongside resistor temperature or power dissipation) to judge
[[thermal-stability]] in the [[operating-duty-test]] (§7.5.6) — thermal stability
requires this quantity to steadily decrease over the last 15 min of U_c\* voltage
application. The resistive component is also the basis of the
[[reference-current]] / [[reference-voltage]] measurement (§2.34–2.35).
